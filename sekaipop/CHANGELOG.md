# Changelog

## Unreleased
- Lockscreen: the keyboard layout you are typing in (EN, RU, ...) is shown as a small tag on the corner of the password plate, in both lockscreen styles, so a password does not fail because the wrong layout was active. It shows only when more than one layout is configured (Settings > Hyprland > Keyboard), like the bar's layout pill; both read one `KeyboardLayoutService` now, so they always agree (issue #110).
- Plugins: get them and keep them up to date from Settings. About / Update > Get plugins lists the reviewed plugins (plugins.json, read by a person before a plugin goes on it), searched by name, word or tag, and installs one with a key, showing the clone's progress and then the next step (turn its bar entry on, place its widget, its settings); From a URL reads any other plugin repository first and installs it only on Install it anyway. About / Update > Plugins says which have updates and shows the commits each brings, updates one or all, goes back to the commit before the last update, pins one where it is, and removes one (keeping its saved settings unless you say). Plugins linked to a folder of your own, or copied in by hand, are never touched. A notification says when plugins have updates, under the shell's own update rules (Tell me about updates, Look every); Desktop > Plugins' rows say where each came from. Everything is in ~/.config/sekaipop/plugins-lock.json, so `ipc call plugins restore` puts them all back on a new machine. `ipc call plugins install | check | update | goback | pin | remove | restore | open` (docs/PLUGIN-INSTALL.md).
- App fonts: pick the font of your GTK, Qt and KDE apps and their monospace font, and the sizes, in Settings > Desktop > App fonts (type part of a name, pick it from the matches). Open apps change at once, and a font picked here is put back at every start if something else changed it. A font set by hand often seemed to reset because each kind of app reads it from a different place: with SekaiPop's `QT_QPA_PLATFORMTHEME=kde`, Qt apps read `~/.config/kdeglobals` and never qt6ct's fonts, and GTK on Wayland reads GNOME's `font-name` over `settings.ini`. The section writes all of them (docs/APP-FONTS.md has the table). `ipc call fonts ui "Noto Sans" 10`, `ipc call fonts status` (issue #98).
- Plugins: a plugin can put an entry in the bar, with a card that opens under it, as well as (or instead of) a desktop widget. The shell draws the entry's pill in every bar style and on every edge, holds it pressed while its card is open, and opens the card like the built-in popups (one at a time, a click away or Esc closes it); the plugin draws only what is inside. The entry is one more module: Settings > Bar > Modules lists it, marked as a plugin with a key to its settings, and Edit Desktop's BAR tab moves it. It is off until you turn it on there. An optional `Service.qml` fetches once for the widget, the entry on every monitor and the card. `ipc call plugins bar <id> on | off | toggle`, `ipc call plugins popup <id>` (for a keybind). The theme names a plugin may rely on now include the bar's family (`surface`, `ink`, `line`, ...) and `warnFill`, the Updates badge's amber (docs/PLUGINS.md; example in examples/plugins/hello-bar).
- Idle & sleep: choose after how long the screen locks, the screen turns off and the machine sleeps, and whether it suspends or hibernates; each can be Never. Settings > General > Idle & sleep (out of the box 5, 10 and 15 minutes, as before). Keep Awake and a playing video still hold all three off. hypridle still does the waiting: the shell writes its times to `~/.config/sekaipop/idle.conf`, which the new `hypridle.conf` reads, and restarts it. Update now doesn't touch the Hyprland config, so on an existing install the section shows **Use these times**, which comments out your `hypridle.conf`'s own listeners (a copy is kept as `hypridle.conf.sekaipop-bak`) and adds the line that reads the file; running install.sh again does it too. `ipc call options set idle.lock 10`, `ipc call idle status` (issue #101; docs/IDLE.md).
- Keybinds: a bare Super tap opens Apps again only when Super was tapped on its own. Since Hyprland 0.57, every Super combination (SUPER + N, SUPER + W, ...) also opened Apps the moment Super came up. The tap is a release bind now, which Hyprland drops when another bind ran while Super was held; a tap moved to Alt or Ctrl in Settings > Hyprland > Keybinds is written the same way. Update now doesn't touch the Hyprland config, so on an existing install run install.sh again, or add `release = true, ` before `description` on the two `SUPER + SUPER_L` / `SUPER + SUPER_R` lines in `~/.config/hypr/hyprland/keybinds.lua` and `hyprctl reload`. A tap you moved in the keybinds panel is fixed by setting it again there (issue #99).
- Power rail: hovering a pill (or arrowing to it) shows what it does, beside the pill on the side away from the phone, so Sleep, Hibernate and Reboot to firmware can be told apart. On Shut down / Reboot, while something is installing or downloading, the same label turns orange and says what it would cut off; that note used to sit on the phone's side, where the phone clipped all but a sliver of it (issue #93).
- Apps: the Apps screen can list the apps you open most first. Settings > General > Launcher & lock > Apps order: A to Z (the default) or Most used, where apps you have opened lately come first and the rest follow A to Z. Use fades over a couple of weeks, so an app you stopped using drops back. With Most used on, it also decides between two equally good search matches. Counts apps opened from the Apps screen; the order moves the next time the screen opens. `ipc call options set launcher.order used` (issue #94; docs/APPS-ORDER.md).
- Keybinds: SUPER + A only opens Apps. It was also bound to Keep Awake, so opening Apps with it flipped Keep Awake every time. Keep Awake now ships without a key: give it one in Settings > Hyprland > Keybinds (Toggle Keep Awake), where Toggle power options (SUPER + Escape) is listed now too. The three test-notification keys (SUPER + ALT + F11 / F12 / =) are gone; `~/.config/hypr/hyprland/scripts/notify-test.sh image | actions | urgent` still sends them. Update now doesn't touch the Hyprland config, so on an existing install run install.sh again, or delete those lines from `~/.config/hypr/hyprland/keybinds.lua` (issue #92).
- Hyprland: window blur works again. The shipped `rules.lua` turned blur off for every window (`no_blur` on `class = ".*"`), so Settings > Hyprland > Blur and `decoration.blur.enabled` did nothing for transparent apps. The rule is gone; the shell's own surfaces are layers and stay unblurred. Update now doesn't touch the Hyprland config, so on an existing install delete that line from `~/.config/hypr/hyprland/rules.lua` (or run install.sh again) and `hyprctl reload` (issue #69).
- Quick Panel: a phone that is only up for a notification no longer shows the banner's Change / Frame keys, the avatar's Frame key or their remove badges when the pointer passes over them on the way to the card. They come back once you click the phone and keep it.
- Lockscreen: optional fingerprint unlock via PAM (`pam_fprintd.so`), gated under Settings > General > Launcher & lock > Fingerprint unlock (off by default).
- Bar: hovering names things. The mic, screen snip, record and the other utility keys, the battery, CPU / RAM, weather, date, keyboard layout and notification bell each show a small hover tag (what they are, and the number: `Battery: 82% · charging`, `CPU 12% · RAM 48%`, `2 notifications`) without a click needed; the Wi-Fi / Bluetooth / speaker and privacy tags keep working. A press still puts a tag away until you leave and come back. The tag's stuck-pointer check also knew the wrong place for a bottom or right bar's window, which could take the tag away as soon as it appeared; it now knows, and never hides a tag from a pointer that is moving over the module.
- Notifications: low-battery warnings on a laptop. A notification at 20 % and 15 % ("Battery low", plug in soon) and at 10 % and 5 % ("Battery very low" / "Battery critical", urgent, so they break through do-not-disturb and Quiet while fullscreen), once each per discharge; plugging in starts over. Settings > General > Time & alerts > Low battery warnings switches them off. Nothing runs on a desktop with no battery. Test without draining it: `ipc call battery simulate 19 | 14 | 9 | 4 | off`, `ipc call battery preview 20 | 15 | 10 | 5`, `ipc call battery status`.
- Updates restart the shell instead of reloading it. Update now, and install.sh when the shell is already running, start a new process on the new files; a reload inside the old one could keep failing with "Failed to load configuration ... FaceKey is not a type" until the shell was killed by hand (issue #79). install.sh used to say a running shell reloads its files by itself, which it doesn't once the old folder is moved aside. The reload error card has a **Restart shell** button for the same case (`scripts/system/restart-shell.sh`).
- About / Update: Update now works when the shell folder, or a folder above it (a `~/.config` kept in a dotfiles repo), is a symlink. It updates the real folder, so the links keep pointing at the new files; it used to refuse with a message the button cut off. When an update doesn't apply, the reason is shown in full under the status (issue #21).
- Plugins: the theme names a plugin may rely on now include what drawing a key like the shell's own takes: `pSurfaceDivider`, `pRingWhite`, `pSurfaceRing`, `pLineShadow` and `shadowOffset` (docs/PLUGINS.md).
- Plugins: `host.reloadSecrets()` reads a plugin's secret files again, so a widget's own "check again" finds a token put in place while the shell runs; `ipc call plugins reload` reads them again too (docs/PLUGINS.md).
- Desktop Focus Mode: the widgets no longer take the keyboard as they pop back in on an empty workspace, so a launcher opened right after (Rofi) can be typed into at once. To-Do, Calendar, Palette and Sticky Notes ask for the keyboard only while the pointer is on them or you are typing in them (issue #72).
- Quick Panel: removing the banner or avatar picture takes two clicks. The first turns the badge into "Remove?", the second removes it; moving away or waiting 3 seconds leaves the picture alone. A stray click on the badge used to delete the picture for good.
- Edit Desktop opens on the monitor you are using: asked for while it is open on another monitor (one you may not be looking at), it moves to yours instead of doing nothing. Toggling it on the monitor it is on still closes it.
- Wallpapers › Wallhaven: the sort (Top and its period, Latest, Views, Favourites, Random) and the filters are kept again; every choice snapped back to the default because their settings had lost their defaults in a merge.
- Pictures: the banner, the avatar and the phone wallpaper can be framed: drag the picture to choose which part shows, scroll to zoom. They are all in one place now, at the front of Edit Desktop › THEME (change, frame, remove, and what the phone shows); the Quick Panel's banner and avatar have Change and Frame keys on hover, a picture you have just picked opens the framer, and the framing shows on the user card and both phone screens too. Presets carry it with the picture. `ipc call pictures pick | frame | unframe | close` (docs/PICTURE-FRAMING.md).
- Settings > Desktop > Plugins: every installed plugin, whether it is on the desktop and on which monitors, which secrets it found (never their values, only where to put a missing one), and the folders that did not load, in plain words with who has to fix them. Open a plugin to change its settings with the shell's own controls; the widget redraws as you go, and a changed setting can be reset. A plugin can describe its settings with the optional `settingsInfo` (labels, groups, ranges, choices) and `groups`; one without still gets a form. A selected plugin widget in Edit Desktop has a Settings key that opens it there (issue #30; docs/PLUGIN-SETTINGS.md).
- Edit Desktop: pull a widget up off the shelf and drop it where it should go. It follows the pointer as big as it will be on the card and lands centred where you let go; one already on the desktop moves there. Back on the shelf, out on the dark stage or Esc puts it back. Clicking still adds, and dragging sideways still scrolls the shelf (docs/SHELF-DRAG.md).
- Desktop widget plugins: a widget can live in its own folder (its own repo) in `~/.config/sekaipop/plugins/` and plug in. The shell places it, drags it, copies it to other monitors, hides it in Focus Mode and keeps it in presets like the built-in widgets; the plugin only draws, and gets the theme, its settings and its secrets (API tokens from the environment or a file, never saved by the shell) through one `host` object. A plugin that is broken or written for another plugin api is skipped with one notification. `ipc call plugins list | reload | toggle | set | resetPosition` (issue #30; docs/PLUGINS.md; example in examples/plugins/hello-clock).
- Themes: one theme per monitor (Settings > Desktop). With it on, the theme picker sets the monitor it is open on; the bar and its popups, the dock, the widgets and the Quick Panel on that monitor wear it, and a monitor on Matugen takes its colours from its own wallpaper. The first monitor sets the shell's theme (apps, lockscreen); picking its theme on another monitor makes that one follow it again (issue #29; docs/PER-MONITOR-THEME.md).
- Phone: the home screen (Quick Panel) has a wallpaper too, behind see-through cards. Settings > General > Quick Panel > Phone wallpaper: Off, Desktop (the monitor's wallpaper) or A picture of your own, the same on the Quick and Apps screens (it replaces the Apps screen's on / off switch, whose choice carries over). An empty notifications card says so, in one quiet line (issue #41; docs/PHONE-WALLPAPER.md).
- Privacy badge: an app recording what is playing (a screen recorder with sound, a VNC or streaming audio feed) shows as desktop audio, with its own icon, instead of "Microphone in use"; muting the mic no longer hides it, since that doesn't stop it.
- Notifications: the phone that rises for a notification can be put away without opening it: swipe it down or middle-click it (the notification stays in the list), or right-click it to remove the notification too, anywhere on the phone. A click, or resting the pointer on its card for a second (it was a fifth of a second, which opened it under anyone aiming at it), still opens the panel (issue #42).
- Phone: clicking beside the Quick Panel's phone closes it everywhere, including the empty corners of the box around the tilted phone, which used to swallow the click. The click follows the phone's real outline, in 2.5D and 2D (issue #47; docs/PHONE-CLICK-AWAY.md).
- Wallpapers: Wallhaven as a second source (My folder | Wallhaven, or Ctrl+Tab). Search it, sort it (Top over a day to a year, Latest, Views, Favourites, Random), filter it (categories, shape, minimum size, colour), and set a picture: it is downloaded into your wallpaper folder first, with progress, and Esc stops it. SFW only, no account. `ipc call wallpaperpicker wallhaven` opens it there (docs/WALLHAVEN.md).
- Edit Desktop > THEME: the Matugen theme has controls. Click its tile again (or its tune key) for the scheme, which wallpaper colour leads, the contrast, and pins that keep an accent colour fixed whatever the wallpaper says; the shell re-colours live, presets carry them, Esc goes back. `ipc call desktopedit matugen` opens them directly. The tile says so when matugen isn't installed (issue #28; docs/MATUGEN-CONTROLS.md).
- Presets: wearing a preset can be undone. The look it replaced (settings, pictures, image frames and sticky notes) is kept, and an UNDO tile on the PRESETS tab puts it back, then turns into REDO. Deleting a preset can be undone too, for ten minutes (UNDELETE, the last one deleted). The save tile says NOT SAVED when no preset is the current look. IPC: `presets undo`, `presets undelete`.
- Keybinds panel: change what each SekaiPop shortcut does, on a keyboard slab (it replaces Settings > Keys). Board view: hold Super / Ctrl / Alt / Shift to see what is on every key, click a key to pick its action. List view: the actions by group, with a search. Pick an action, Press keys, then press the new keys or click a key on the board; it works straight away, conflicts ask first, and every change can be reset. Open it from Settings > Hyprland > Keybinds or `ipc call keybinds toggle | show | hide`. The bare Super tap that opens Apps is an ordinary row ("Open apps"), not a fixed one: clear it to free Super, or tap Alt or Ctrl instead. Works with the Lua config and with `hyprland.conf` (issue #16).
- Phone: the turn between the Quick Panel and Settings / Wallpapers (both ways) is smooth instead of a snap: one 200 ms speed for every turn of the phone (Settings, Wallpapers, Apps), but on a soft curve, with the two screens dissolving over about 70 - 100 ms instead of swapping in 3 frames, and the turn waits for the phone to be redrawn before it moves so a slow first frame does not skip it.
- Phone: the slide between Quick and Apps is the same 200 ms, softer, with a visible cross-fade.
- Phone: SUPER + N while the Settings (or Wallpapers) are up turns the phone into the Quick Panel, whichever way the Settings were opened; it used to cut or close them.
- Apps: the calculator, clipboard, colour, emoji and web cards fade in over the grid (and out again) instead of replacing it in one frame; system-action rows fade and the grid glides to make room; the card is built before the slide starts and stays until the screen has gone.
- Dock: Reveal zone setting (Auto-hide / Smart hide); pinned apps can be switched on or off in Edit Desktop > DOCK > APPS.
- Notifications: clicking runs the app's default action (Discord opens the message); middle click hides, right click removes; Quiet while fullscreen option.
- Apps: two-line names, a letter icon for apps with no icon, the list refreshes when the Apps screen opens.
- Shadows: tiled windows get shadows again with a stronger default; the phone casts a soft, airbrushed shadow (one small picture, no blur); Settings > Quick > Phone shadow switches it off.
- Lockscreen: optional lyrics under the clock (gacha style; Settings > General).
